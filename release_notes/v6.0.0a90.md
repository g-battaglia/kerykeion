# v6.0.0a90 — Three sizes for the planet cluster, and what each one costs

One body of work: the modern wheel's planet cluster — glyph, degrees, sign,
minutes, retrograde mark — now comes in three sizes, selected by a new
`glyph_size` option on `ChartDrawer` (`"small"`, `"medium"`, `"large"`;
constructor default, overridable per render, exported as `KerykeionGlyphSize`).
The interesting part is not the option. It is that the wheel had no room to
sell, so every size had to be *derived* — and the derivation had to say
honestly what it was spending.

---

## Medium is not a size, it is the contract

`"medium"` IS the current drawing. The medium profile does not restate the
renderer's constants, it references them, and the default render is asserted
byte-identical to a render that never heard of the option — string for string,
and across the entire pre-existing SVG baseline suite, which passes untouched.
A chart drawn today is the same bytes it was before this release; the option
only exists where it is asked for.

## Small is the medium cluster at 90%

A pure homothety. The five element sizes scale by 0.9, and so do all the
quantities of air — the clearance from the tether's end to the glyph's corner,
the four ink-to-ink gaps between the rows, the bottom margin, the tether's own
tab. The cluster keeps exactly the proportions it was tuned to, just smaller,
with the spare band left where it falls.

## Large is classic parity, and the air pays for it

`"large"` draws the planet glyph at the classic style's own size: the classic
engine draws its 24-unit glyph box at scale 1.0 on a single wheel and 0.8 on a
dual — 24px and 19.2px at the default 480px page — while the modern wheel
lives behind a 0.92 zodiac-ring wrapper and a 4.8 page scale. The large planet
base is *written* as `classic_scale / (0.92 · 4.8)`, so the parity is exact by
construction and asserted as a string match on the emitted SVG, not as a
tolerance on a number. Parity is the GLYPH's contract: on the dual rings the
reading scales by the single wheel's factor instead (next section).

Exact, with two qualifiers the docs state rather than imply. Parity holds for
glyphs at optical weight 1.0 — the per-glyph map stays applied, so the Sun
draws 10% over and the ×0.95 bodies 5% under, exactly as they always have
relative to their own row. And parity is a default-configuration contract:
`show_zodiac_background_ring=False` removes the 0.92 wrapper and scales the
whole modern wheel — cluster included, at every size — up by 1/0.92, so a
ring-less large glyph lands at 26.09px against classic's 24. The cluster
keeps its proportions to its own wheel either way; a test pins the ring-less
factor so it stays a stated behaviour rather than a surprise.

The cost had to come from somewhere. Measured against the browser-derived ink
tables, the cluster's ink already occupied 64.6% of the natal band and 67.5% /
69.2% of the two dual bands: there was no reserve to grow into, and the bands
cannot deepen — the natal ring ends at the house ring, and below the dual
rings there is only the aspect web, whose radius would have had to give up a
third of itself. That was considered and refused.

So the growth is paid out of the air between the rows. The single ring affords
parity at 76% of its medium air — denser, still legible: the tightest gap of
the natal cluster, glyph to degrees, narrows from 0.92 to 0.70 wheel units
(4.4px to 3.4px at the default page).

On the dual rings the factors split, and the split was judged where it
belongs — on rendered wheels, four candidates side by side (×1.372 shared,
×1.248 the single wheel's ramp, ×1.12, ×1.0). One factor for everything made
the dual degree numerals LARGER than the single wheel's — 12.9px against 12.3
— because the dual cluster is text-heavy by construction: its reading stands
at 0.67 of its glyph where the natal stands at 0.51. Even the single wheel's
ramp read oversized in the dual rings' packed context. So at large the dual
glyph alone takes the parity factor, and the reading — degrees, sign,
minutes, ℞ — **stays at the medium size**: a large dual wheel grows its
glyphs to classic parity and keeps the reading it always had. The single
wheel splits the same way one step down: its degrees keep the ×1.248 ramp
while minutes and ℞ stay at the medium size — the coda was what crowded,
not the ramp. The choice hands the air back too: the dual rings afford
large at 79% (outer) and 73% (inner) of their medium air, with inner gaps
of 3.4–4.6px — against the 41% / 23% and 1.0–1.3px that one factor for
everything had left them. The
separation ceilings barely move, and for a telling reason: with the reading
at medium size, the BINDING row of a large dual ring is the parity glyph
itself, not the text. The CHANGELOG
says the same thing, because a release note that hides the trade is how a
future reader mistakes a decision for a bug.

## One rule, and the tether inside it

The small and large layouts are the output of a new script,
`scripts/derive_modern_cluster_profiles.py`, under one documented rule: sizes
scale by `k`; every quantity of air scales by `a = min(k, fit)`, where `fit`
fills the band exactly; rows are laid top-down from the tether's end; and the
tether's tab never drops below a quarter unit — when the floor binds (it
binds exactly once, on the large dual-inner ring), the fit is re-solved with
the tab pinned.

Two details carry more weight than they look like they do. The layout anchors
on the glyph's **corner** reach, not its half-height, because the tether runs
along the cluster's radius and meets an axis-aligned box on the diagonal —
the same geometry whose neglect a88 had to repair when tethers ended inside
the Sun's box at 45°. And the tab scales with the air, which is precisely
what keeps that repair true at every size.

One more move sits on top of the rule, judged on the rendered wheels: every
derived cluster slides 0.3 units **outward**, toward its indicator, on every
chart type — the ℞ row was sitting on the ring's inner edge, and the slack to
buy it back lives on the tether's side. The tether's END segment absorbs the
slide, floored at a quarter unit and capped where the floor binds so it never
reaches the glyph's own ink — the cap is what saves the small dual-inner
ring, whose tab had no slack left to give. The VISIBLE dash is a separate
length now (`start_tick_length`): the straight tether's whole body and the
arc's mark at the true position grow back toward the MEDIUM dash length,
limited only by the glyph's corner budget — the natal large dash stands at
1.02 against its end tab's 0.40, and the dual-inner dashes stay at the floor
because their corner budget is negative by construction. A dash cut down
with the end segment read as a stray dot on the wheel — Giacomo's call, both
times, on the rendered chart. Medium, the byte-identity anchor, does not
move.

The rule is a fixed point at `k = 1`: on every run the script re-derives the
shipped medium from its own decomposition and refuses to continue if it
cannot. The pasted profile literals are pinned to the derivation by test —
change the policy and the numbers stop matching; change a number and the
policy disowns it. Neither can drift alone.

## The ceilings are measured, because the ink is real

Cluster separations are capped per ring by measured ceilings, and a bigger
cluster needs bigger ones. The measurement harness
(`scripts/measure_modern_separation.py`) learned `--glyph-size` and now
builds its rings from the same profiles the renderer reads, so the bench
cannot drift from the drawing; its sweep extends to 14°, because the large
dual-inner ring touches past where the old sweep ended.

Re-measuring exposed a pleasant fact: the shipped medium ceilings sit exactly
on the harness's "first safe with 0.25 units of daylight" column — the policy
was already written in the constants, it had just never been named. The new
ceilings apply that policy to their own floors: small 7.00° / 5.25° / 8.00°,
large 9.50° / 7.75° / 12.50° (ink touches at 6.50 / 4.75 / 7.25 and
9.00 / 7.25 / 11.75). Adversarial mode — mixed content, the renderer's own
placement — passes at every size against the 0.2-unit daylight gate. An
all-points wheel at large asks for more arc than a circle has, by design; the
resolver compresses and logs it, and a test pins the log line so the
degradation stays a stated behaviour rather than a surprise.

## For consumers holding only the SVG

A small or large wheel stamps `kr:glyphsize` on its modern root
(`ModernHoroscope` / `ModernDualHoroscope`), so hit-area injection and other
downstream consumers can tell which profile drew the pixels in front of them.
Medium is unstamped on purpose: the attribute's absence IS the default, which
is the same byte-identity contract stated above.

## Upgrading from a88

Nothing to do. The option defaults to `"medium"`, the default render is
byte-identical, and the classic style ignores `glyph_size` in silence — the
same convention as `show_zodiac_background_ring`. To opt in:

```python
ChartDrawer(chart_data, glyph_size="large").generate_svg_string()   # instance default
drawer.generate_svg_string(style="modern", glyph_size="small")      # per render
```

Eight new SVG baselines cover the small and large wheels; the 346 chart
baselines that existed before this release (and the 29 gallery fixtures beside
them) are byte-for-byte unchanged.
