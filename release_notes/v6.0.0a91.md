# v6.0.0a91 — Five things the sky said and the library didn't

All five came from the same place: a downstream screen where two numbers from
this library sat next to each other and disagreed. A Moon at 100% illumination
called "Waning Gibbous". A Sun in Virgo filed under a nakshatra that belongs to
Leo. A Mercury 1.8° from the Sun presented as an evening star. A Moon with a
"perihelion". Four fields for moonrise that had never held a value. None of
them was a computation error — every number was right. What was wrong was the
word attached to it, or the word that was missing.

---

## The phase name is centred on the event

`calculate_moon_phase` has always cut the Sun–Moon separation into 28 bins of
12.857° and named the four major phases by bin. The bins were never centred:
Full Moon was [167.14°, 180.00°) — the twelve degrees *before* the opposition
and none after it — so the first minute past the true full moon read "Waning
Gibbous" while the illumination, which is continuous, still read 100%. New
Moon had the mirror defect on the other side of the conjunction; the quarters
sat 6.43° off their instant in opposite directions.

The library carried the proof of this in its own tests. The 365 historical
syzygies pinned in `test_moon_phase_historical_verification.py` include 95
that were named wrong, and the test accepted the wrong neighbour by design:
`{"Full Moon", "Waning Gibbous"}` was a passing answer for a full moon. A
fixture built as a new moon, at 359.99°, reported "Waning Crescent".

The names now come from windows **centred** on the syzygy or quadrature, with
exactly the widths they had before — New and Full Moon ±6.4286°, First and
Last Quarter ±19.2857°, the four intermediate phases filling the rest. That
is the smallest change that makes the words true: 365 of 365 syzygies name
correctly, 14.29% of the circle changes name, and the 1–28 `moon_phase` index
(the lunation day drawn on the wheel) does not move at all. `LunarPhaseModel`
gains `major_phase` and `stage`, the two fields `MoonPhaseDetailsFactory`
already had, and both surfaces now read one definition.

What it cost: 24 SVG baselines and one report fixture, each changed by
exactly one line — verified by regenerating the whole surface before and
after and diffing at 1e-4.

One of those lines is worth naming. On the two French Jeanne Moreau charts
the natal panel's phase row went from `Nouvelle Lune` to `Premier
Croissant`, and the longer name no longer fits the slot the wheel leaves it:
`153.8px` against a `153.65px` budget. The existing trimmer does what it has
always done and the row reads `Phase Lunaire: Premier Croiss…`. No mechanism
is new here. 9 of the 80 locale × phase combinations that row can hold
overrun the same budget — this one by 0.15px, the least of them; the French,
Spanish and Portuguese gibbous names and two Russian ones run 1 to 50px
further past it, and the ellipsis has been absorbing them all along. What
changed is only which phase these two charts name. A wider slot, or shorter
translations, is separate work.

## Nakshatras on a tropical chart

The 27 nakshatras divide the sidereal zodiac. Asking for them on a tropical
chart used to run the division on the tropical longitudes as they were, ~24°
off, and log a warning that no HTTP client would ever see. The factory now
rotates the longitudes by an ayanamsa for the division only —
`nakshatra_ayanamsa`, default `"LAHIRI"` — and the chart stays tropical. The
Sun of 2026-08-28 in Virgo 4°55′ reads Magha, as it should, instead of
Uttara Phalguni. Sidereal charts are untouched; `nakshatra_ayanamsa=None`
gives the old values back, with the warning.

## Moonrise and moonset

Four fields on `MoonPhaseMoonSummaryModel`, four lines in the XML serialiser,
and no producer. They are filled now, for the subject's civil day, in the
subject's zone, `None` on the day in thirty when the Moon does not rise (or
set). The Sun's routine is generalised by a `body` argument and its old name
is kept, byte-identical.

## "Evening star" said where, not whether

`is_evening_star` means *east of the Sun* and nothing else; it always did,
and it still does. The new `solar_phase` says whether the body can be seen —
cazimi, combust, under the beams, free — from the elongation the model already
publishes, against thresholds that are parameters (the tradition disagrees on
all three) and are echoed on the result so nobody has to guess which
convention named the label.

## The Moon's apogee

`PlanetaryNodesFactory` called the Moon's far apsis its aphelion. It is the
apogee, and to the last decimal it is the mean (or, osculating, the true)
Black Moon Lilith the rest of the library already computes. `periapsis`,
`apoapsis` and `apsis_kind` say it plainly; the old names stay and hold the
same objects.
