# v6.0.0a87 — The house you clicked

a86 is on PyPI and this tree has moved past it. That is the first reason for a new
number: the wheel a86 ships is not the wheel this tree draws, and the license file
the NOTICE points at never made it into the a86 wheel at all — PyPI does not allow
a version to be replaced, so the fix can only reach anyone under a new one.

What changed since is one subject with several faces: **which house you are
looking at, and which one answers when you click it.**

## Six house systems draw their houses backwards

Above roughly 68 degrees Campanus, Regiomontanus, Sunshine, Polich/Page and APC
return their cusps in descending order, and the horizon system does it on the
equator. Counted over 32,844 charts — all 23 systems, half a degree of latitude at
a time, four times of day — six reverse outright and two more go degenerate.

Read forwards, which is all the drawing code knew how to do, a six-degree house
measures 354. The twelve then wind round the wheel eleven times instead of once,
so every one of the twelve transparent hit-wedges was painted as a near-complete
ring: stacked, invisible, each declaring `pointer-events: all`, so a click
anywhere on the chart was answered by whichever was drawn last. The house numbers
went with them, each centred half of 354 degrees along — the far side of the wheel
from the house it names.

Direction is now read once from all twelve, because it cannot be decided pair by
pair: a single house may legitimately run past 180 degrees, which Placidus manages
at high latitude, and taking the shorter arc there would cut it in half. Twelve
widths cover the circle exactly once whichever way the houses run, so the total
tells the two apart — 360 one way, 3960 the other.

## Cusps that cross, and the house with no target

Polich/Page inside the polar circle, and Sunshine/alt, return cusps that are not
ordered at all: one house runs back while the next few run on, so the houses
genuinely overlap and nothing can tile. There each wedge is held to at least one
degree, moving only its own end — overlapping its neighbour, which is honest,
because the cusps overlap.

Two cusps inside one whole degree collapse onto one offset when the classic ring
is quantised, and an arc whose endpoints coincide is dropped by the SVG spec: a
path of no area still declaring `pointer-events: all` is a house nobody can ever
click. They are separated in house order now, so the boundaries stay shared —
wedge i ends exactly where wedge i+1 begins — and three coincident cusps come
apart as readily as two.

Swept over 690 charts, every house system, both engines: no wedge of zero area,
none under the minimum.

## House numbers, measured where they are actually drawn

A label's reach in degrees is its reach in pixels over the arc a degree covers, so
the radius goes straight into the answer. It was being taken at the radius where
the cusp *line* ends rather than where the number is written — 120 against 192 on
a natal wheel, 80 against 156 on a dual chart's inner ring — which made every
extent 1.6x too large, and 1.95x on that inner ring. Per-label extents exist to
stop a crowd being walked out of its own houses; measured there they walked it
further than the uniform figure they replaced.

Two more of the same family: the inner ring took its base from the truncated
offsets its lines are drawn from and its span from the exact positions, so where
two cusps share a degree the fractions decided the order and the wheel read **10
before 9, and 4 before 3**; and the outer ring of a dual chart labelled lines that
keep their fraction with numbers built on a truncated base.

## The composite chart gets a cusp ring it can have

About one couple in sixteen got twelve cusps that were not a house division.
Between two points on a circle there are two midpoints, half a turn apart, and
taking the nearer one for each of the twelve independently breaks when the two
charts' angles are nearly opposed: the choice flips partway round the ring and the
twelve arcs come to 1080 degrees instead of 360.

The trade the profession settles on is to hold one angle at its near midpoint and
move the others onto their far midpoint as the order requires — Solar Fire's
"long-arc midpoints instead of short-arc", Kepler's "flipping the houses 180
degrees if necessary", Townley's rule for the stray cusp and its opposite. The new
`house_anchor` argument chooses which angle is held: `auto` (the default, and Solar
Fire's — whichever of the Ascendant and Midheaven has its base cusps closer
together), `ascendant`, or `midheaven`. A ring that already runs in order comes
back untouched, value for value.

Where both charts' houses run the same way the repaired ring covers the circle
exactly once, always. Where one partner's houses run backwards and the other's
forwards there is no direction for the composite to run in, and that is said out
loud rather than shipped quietly.

The composite also stopped keeping a private copy of the library's house reader.
The copy measured every house as the arc running forwards from its cusp, so on a
descending ring it filed ten points out of ten wrongly — while the same model's
house-comparison field, which used the shared function, disagreed with it.

The four angles hang from the same frame as the cusps, and only from it. Read off
the cusps instead and the composite Ascendant depends on the house system, by
half a turn; averaged on their own and they part company with a repaired ring,
leaving a Midheaven a quarter of the circle from its own Ascendant.

A frame cannot be hung between every pair. Two charts whose houses run opposite
ways are the usual reason — one partner inside the polar circle under a reversing
system, the other outside it — but not the only one: a parent whose own cusps are
not ordered at all does it too, and so, rarely, do two plain rings running the
same way.

And placing a ring on a frame does not by itself produce twelve houses. Neither
parent need be degenerate for that: Campanus at 75N has twelve distinct cusps
winding once backwards, and the equatorial chart it is paired with winds once
forwards; it is the placing that collapses them, landing five antipodal pairs on
each other — cusp 4 on cusp 10, and the Midheaven then sitting on two cusps at
once. So the ring is placed, then checked, and where the check fails the answer
is the plain midpoints with the frame marked incoherent. Nothing rotates a ring
that has no frame, either: the rotation exists to make a framed ring agree with
its frame, and on a ring that is not on one it only slides the cusp out from
under an angle that cannot follow it.

Where the parents put an angle on a cusp, the composite records which house that
angle opens instead of handing the longitude back to a reader that has to find it
again — because on a ring with two cusps at one longitude the reader answers with
the earlier of the two, and a Midheaven that IS the tenth cusp gets filed in the
fourth.

Above the polar circle the composite inherits one split it cannot repair: the
reversing systems report the *parent's* own Midheaven half a turn from where the
others put it, so two composites cast under different systems disagree about the
Midheaven because their parents already did.

`CompositeSubjectModel` gains `house_anchor`, recording which angle was held.
`None` on a Davison chart, which never needs one.

## The Gauquelin ring was drawn off its own wheel

A Gauquelin chart replaces the twelve house wedges with thirty-six sector ones,
and those cusps descend by construction. The modern engine corrected its span for
that and left both sweep flags where the ascending case put them — the one
combination that moves an arc onto the mirrored circle SVG puts through any two
points at a given radius. Measured on the shipped gallery: the thirty-six wedges
sat on circles up to 92 units from a wheel whose radius is 50. The classic
engine's twin had always used the flipped pair.

## The document says which house division it is describing

A chart asked for in Placidus above the polar circle is cast in Porphyry, and the
substitution is recorded. Both the text report and `to_context` printed only the
system actually used, so every house sentence written off that context described a
division the caller did not choose. The report now reads
`Porphyry (substituted for Placidus)`, and the context carries a
`<polar_house_fallback>` element. The composite's `house_anchor` reaches both as
well.

Degrees printed beside a sign are bounded by that sign's ceiling rather than by
360: 149.99687 was printing as `150.00` — zero degrees of Virgo — on a row
labelled Leo, and a cusp could print as `30.00`, a degree that does not exist.

## The composite says whether its anchor was actually held

`house_anchor` is a request. Where the two charts admit no common frame it grants
nothing — all three anchors return the same ring — and on a ring with gaps every
house name is the house whose cusp the point last passed rather than a house
containing the point. A chart carrying only the request described a construction
that had not happened, and the report and the context repeated it as fact.

`CompositeSubjectModel` gains **`house_frame`**, which says which of three things
the twelve cusps are: `"anchored"` (a frame was hung from the requested angle and
the twelve cover the circle once), `"midpoints"` (no frame, but the twelve are
still a house division) or `"gapped"` (no frame, and they are not). On the latter
two every position is its own near midpoint, with one exception: a cusp the
parents put exactly opposite another is kept opposite it, because a symmetric
mean cannot tell an antipodal pair from its own reverse and would otherwise put
the fourth cusp on the tenth. `None` on a
Davison chart. The report's House Anchor row now reads
`midheaven (not held: the twelve cusps are not a house division)` on such a chart,
and the context carries `house_frame` beside `house_anchor`.

A frame is a common reading of two rings, so both have to be rings. A parent
whose own twelve neither tile nor agree on a direction — Polich/Page at 68S
manages 360.198 degrees with mixed directions — could still have a frame placed
on it and come back winding once by accident, and then the anchor, which decides
nothing on such a pair, silently decided everything: held on the Midheaven one
composite came back anchored with its Sun in the second house, held on either
other angle it came back gapped with the Sun in the ninth. Both parents are now
required to be house divisions running the SAME way before any frame is accepted
— both winding once is not enough, since forwards and backwards each wind once,
and a frame spanning one of each measures an arc as a separation in one chart and
as its complement in the other. It costs 2,615 rings
of 258,201 that used to be repaired by accident and are now returned as plain
midpoints, which is what they are.

Two more from the same pass. **`house_anchor="auto"` now measures its two
separations symmetrically** — the folded modulo it used is arithmetic on ordered
inputs, and for a pair exactly half a circle apart it returned 180.0 one way and
179.99999999999997 the other, enough to hold a different angle and turn the whole
ring half a circle. And **the house sectors of a crossed ring are painted widest
first**, so the narrowest is on top — and ties go to the lower house number, the
way the reader breaks them: Polich/Page at 70N puts one longitude inside houses
7, 9 and 12, and a click landed on the twelfth while the model said the ninth. A
ring that tiles is painted in house order exactly as before.

Two smaller ones. `get_planet_house` now reads containment from `house_spans`
where the twelve are a house division, instead of choosing the shorter arc for
each pair on its own — the two disagreed about a ring with a house wider than
half the circle, which no real chart produces (the widest arc measured is
179.2388 degrees) but which one of them accepted and the other raised on. Every
house assignment across 3,685 real charts is unchanged, to the bit. And the
ancillary fallback record a polar Gauquelin call files no longer claims to have
moved `house_cusps` and `angles`: the chart keeps only the 36-sector ring from
that call, and its own cusps and angles are bit-identical with Gauquelin switched
off.

## The composite carries its derived points, like every other chart

A midpoint composite materialised only the points a caller asked for, so the
Descendant, the Imum Coeli and the south node came back `None` whenever they were
not in `active_points` — while an ordinary subject and a Davison chart keep them,
and the context promises a horizon, a meridian and a node section
unconditionally. They are the other end of a point that IS active, and they are
now derived from it at half a turn rather than averaged on their own: two points
half a circle apart are the same unordered pair as their own opposites, so a
symmetric mean hands both the same longitude — a south node whose parents sit at
190 and 10 came out on top of the north node.

The four angles are materialised whatever the preset, the way the context's own
contract already described that section: a composite built with
`["Sun", "Moon", "Mercury", "Venus", "Mars"]` had no Ascendant, no Midheaven and
no `<axes>` block at all, while an ordinary subject built the same way keeps them.
And the opposites come from the registry the subject factory uses rather than a
second list beside it — that copy had never heard of Priapus, so a composite asked
for Lilith came back without one.

`active_points` is unchanged, so display and aspect filters read exactly what they
read before. What changes is that a composite's **Angularities** section is no
longer half empty: a Venus 0.63 degrees from the Descendant now says so.

`CompositeSubjectModel` also stops accepting states it cannot mean:
`composite_chart_type` is typed, and a Davison chart may not carry `house_anchor`
or `house_frame` — a Davison is cast as an ordinary chart and has no frame, and a
payload claiming otherwise had the report printing a House Anchor row for a chart
with no anchor. The converse is deliberately not required: an a86 midpoint
payload carries neither field and still validates.

## Known, measured, and not fixed here
**A click on a crossing house ring is approximate, by design.** A house narrower
than one degree is widened so it can be clicked at all, and a widened wedge covers
longitudes outside its own arc: where it is also the narrowest it is painted on
top and answers for them. On Polich/Page at 70.5N — an eleventh house 0.348
degrees wide — the wheel and the reader disagree across 1.3 degrees of the 360.
Painting widened wedges underneath was tried and is worse: the tenth house crosses
the eleventh there, so the eleventh loses its own middle and goes back to being
unclickable. No single paint order settles both, and the slop is bounded by the
minimum width. A click exactly ON a cusp is a separate matter: an SVG path is
closed, so both wedges sharing a boundary contain it, and no ordering of twelve
cyclic wedges gives every cusp to the house it opens — the reader has an
exact-on-cusp rule and the wheel cannot.


**A natal chart can still file an angle in the wrong house, and a Davison chart
inherits it.** Where a house system brings several cusps onto one longitude —
Sunshine at 74.25N puts the eighth through the twelfth on 74.099142 — the reader
scans the twelve and answers with the earliest, so a Midheaven that is the tenth
cusp comes back in the eighth house. 68 charts of 8,280 measured, all under that
one system, from 89.9S to 84.1N.

It is the same defect the composite has just had fixed, and a87 does not fix it
here: the composite records which house each angle opens because it knows, and
nothing does that where the cusps are first computed. a86 does the same thing, so
nothing regresses — but the composite is now right where an ordinary chart is not,
and that is worth saying rather than leaving to be discovered. The fix belongs
where the cusps are made, in one place rather than in each factory.

## Upgrading from a86

The compile-time surface only grows: no name, signature, model field or
`Literal` member was removed or reordered, nothing became required, and an a86
payload validates on a87 untouched. What is new is optional — the `house_anchor`
constructor argument on `CompositeSubjectFactory`, the field of the same name on
`CompositeSubjectModel` and `house_frame` beside it, with the
`CompositeHouseFrame` literal it uses — all defaulting to the behaviour a caller
who does not know about them already gets. Compared across 247 subject cases and 448
charts — every house system, ten latitudes, four perspectives, five sidereal
modes, four centuries — **no ordinary chart's positions, houses or aspects
change**, and the `kr:` attribute vocabulary is identical.

Four things a consumer will notice, in order of blast radius.

**Midpoint composites read differently, and that is the point.** On 140 ordinary
mid-latitude pairs, 19 come back with their house frame turned half a circle, and
22 have a planet in a different house; on one, the Ascendant itself flips and
takes five points from the first house to the seventh.

Measured against the requirement that twelve cusps be a division of the circle
into twelve houses, over 32,913 a86 composites and the 98,739 a87 ones the three
anchors make of the same pairs: at 65 degrees and below **9.9% of a86's rings are
not a house division, against 2.2% of a87's**; above the polar circle, 19.3%
against 8.9%. What remains in both is pairs no arrangement of midpoints repairs.
Angles: where both parents put the Midheaven on their own tenth cusp, a86 files it
outside the tenth house **637 times above the polar circle and never below it**;
a87, never. The change fires only where a86 was wrong — but a stored a86 composite
re-rendered on a87 can read a planet in the opposite house, and reloading the old
payload does not repair it. The two are distinguishable: an a87 chart carries
`house_anchor`, an a86 one does not.

**Kerykeion no longer configures logging for you.** a86 called `logging.warning`
at module level, which implicitly runs `basicConfig()` and installs a handler on
the root logger. An application that never configured logging saw formatted
kerykeion output because of that accident, and now sees only Python's last-resort
handler — so INFO lines such as "No sidereal mode set" disappear, and anything
keyed on `record.name == "root"` stops matching. Configure the `kerykeion` logger
to get them back.

**The modern wheel-only SVG paints its background.** It was the only one of the
four templates that did not; now all four agree. Pass `transparent_background=True`
for the old behaviour — which is what a consumer compositing the wheel over its
own surface wants.

**An inverted hours range now raises.** `start=12:00, end=11:30, step_type="hours"`
returned one sample outside the window it was asked for; it raises `ValueError`
instead. Forward ranges are byte-identical.

## Notes for anyone building on the SVG

- No `kr:` attribute, node name, `Literal` or schema field was removed or renamed:
  the node vocabulary is 67 values before and 67 after. A consumer reading
  `kr:node="HouseSector"` and `kr:house` needs no change — the wedges it was
  already reading are simply the right shape now.
- `HouseSector` path data changes on charts with reversed or crowded houses, and
  the `large-arc`/`sweep` flags change with it. Both flags move together: SVG puts
  two circles of a given radius through any two points, and it is the pair that
  says which.
- House number positions move on every chart, by a few pixels where nothing was
  crowded and by more where something was.
- `CompositeSubjectModel` gains `house_anchor`, recording which angle was held.
  `None` on a Davison chart, which never needs one.
- `AstrologicalSubjectFactory` writes its log lines to its own module logger
  instead of the root logger, so a host that silences `kerykeion.*` now does.
- `normalize_degree` and `house_spans` moved to `kerykeion.utilities.core`. Both
  are still importable from `kerykeion.charts.utils`.
- The default fixed-star lists spell Deneb Algedi `Deneb_Algedi`, the form used
  everywhere else and the one that has a translation.
- `GauquelinSector` path data changes in the modern engine: the wedges are on the
  wheel now. A consumer reading `kr:node="GauquelinSector"` and `kr:sector` needs
  no change.
- `poe regenerate:glyph-gallery` works again. It had named a stylesheet that went
  with the removed themes, so `regenerate:all` died on it.
