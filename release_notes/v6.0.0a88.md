# v6.0.0a88 — What the point is, what the test could see, and where the moon stands

Three bodies of work, and they turn out to share one shape: **an answer that was
being derived from numbers that could not carry it.** An angle filed by twelve
cusp longitudes that cannot say which cusp it is; a golden test measuring with
tolerances that cannot fail; and a moon disc placed by chart type instead of
beside the line that names it, on a panel whose widths were charged to a script
the estimator had never measured.

---

## The angle that opened a house it was not filed in

a87's notes named this and left it:

> A natal chart can still file an angle in the wrong house, and a Davison chart
> inherits it. […] The fix belongs where the cusps are made, in one place rather
> than in each factory.

Where a house system brings several cusps onto one longitude, the shared reader
answers with the lowest-numbered of them. Sunshine at 74.25 degrees north puts the
second, third, fourth, fifth and sixth cusps on 316.971024; the Imum Coeli **is** the
fourth cusp, bit for bit; the chart said the third — in the model, in the report
and in the context alike.

a87 had already made the exact-on-cusp rule take the *nearest* cusp rather than the
first one it walked past. That settles a crowd whose members differ by a fraction
of a nanodegree, which is most of them. It cannot settle a crowd whose members are
the same float: they are all equally near, and no rule written over twelve numbers
can prefer one.

**The answer is not in the twelve numbers.** It is in what the point IS — and the
ephemeris knows that at the moment it hands back the cusps and the angles from one
call, and nowhere afterwards. So that is where it is now recorded.
`angle_house_identities` states the rule once, in `utilities/core`, beside
`normalize_degree` and `house_spans` which were moved there for the same reason;
it is applied at the two places that produce cusps: the natal factory, through
`houses_ring_with_polar_fallback`, and the relocated chart, which shifts the ring
by the ayanamsa first and then asks the same question of the shifted ring. A third
copy of the houses call, kept in the factory "for the Arabic Parts", turned out
to be unreachable — the houses are always cast first — and is gone.

Nothing else moved. `get_planet_house` behaves exactly as before, and the roughly
twenty other callers were not touched: they project a point into a ring that is not
its own, where no identity exists and none is claimed. Whole sign, Vehlow and
Morinus charts claim none either — there an angle is a point of its own and may
legitimately fall in a neighbouring house, which is what those systems mean; equal
houses claim the Ascendant and Descendant only, meridian and equal-from-MC charts
the Midheaven and Imum Coeli only.

Measured over 5,520 charts — 23 systems, 15 latitudes, 8 hours, 2 minutes — one
angle was misfiled before and none is after. At a quarter of an hour's resolution
across the eight systems that crowd their cusps and fourteen latitudes beyond 66
degrees, some 10,700 charts, the same one case and no other. It is the Imum Coeli
every time, and that is not luck: the reader breaks ties towards the lowest cusp
number, so an Ascendant standing on a crowd always wins by being first and a
Midheaven always loses. The Midheaven's half of the rule is therefore pinned on a
ring written for the purpose rather than left waiting for an ephemeris to produce
one.

### The cusps are not repaired, and that is deliberate

pyswisseph returns the **same crowded ring** for that chart — cusps two through six
on 316.971024, to the digit. It is not a defect in the backend; it is what the
Sunshine construction does there. Substituting Porphyry, as several commercial
programs do at polar latitudes while still printing "Placidus", would diverge from
the implementation this library is validated against.

Worth knowing: Swiss Ephemeris' own `swe_house_pos` answers *sixth* for that Imum
Coeli, while its own cusp array puts the fourth cusp on that longitude. There is no
external answer to copy. Its documented convention is the one already followed — a
point on a cusp belongs to the house that cusp opens — and this release simply lets
the four angles reach it.

### What the chart says instead

`coincident_house_cusps` is new on the subject: the groups of house numbers whose
cusps stand on one longitude, so a reader knows which houses have no width and can
never contain anything. Empty for every ordinary chart, so an a87 payload validates
untouched. The report prints a **Cusps On One Longitude** row; the context emits
`<coincident_house_cusps>`.

## The golden test that could not fail

From a87's notes:

> the suite passed the same without regenerating them, because `compare_svg_lines`
> uses `rel_tol=0.5` […] Not touched here, but it is the next place to look.

It was worse than the tolerance. The comparison **returned without asserting**
whenever a line's count of numbers differed, and again whenever the line differed
anywhere it had no numbers — a different label, a renamed class, a deleted
attribute, a whole element gone. When the file's line count changed it abandoned
the comparison entirely in favour of a ±5% length ratio. A missing baseline skipped.
Only what survived all of that was compared, at fifty per cent relative tolerance:
±150 units on a coordinate of 300.

Three more copies of the same function lived in the test tree with three more
tolerances, one of them with no callers at all while the documentation advertised
it.

There is one comparison now.

- **Structure is fatal, on every backend.** Line count, the count of numbers in a
  line, and the line with its numbers blanked out.
- **Numbers**: `rel_tol=0.0`, `abs_tol=1e-4` — the last digit of a coordinate printed
  at six decimal places, and nothing else; a DMS label that flips by one second
  moves 2.78e-4 and is rejected.
- **Compared only on the backend the baselines came from.** On another, the
  structural assertions still run and the test then reports SKIPPED with the
  reason. Measured on this file's golden tests: structural strictness costs the
  same handful on either backend, numeric strictness costs 155 on swisseph against
  none on libephemeris. Those are not less precise charts, they are different
  ones — different return moments, bodies one backend does not carry — and the
  single tolerance wide enough to cover that is the 0.5 being retired.
- A few charts cast two millennia back differ **structurally** between the
  backends: an aspect falls in or out of orb. Those carry
  `@pytest.mark.reference_backend_only`, one at a time, each with its reason.

### What it found the moment it could fail

**The golden charts were asking a remote service where they were cast.**
`from_birth_data` defaults to `online=True`, and both the tests and the
regeneration scripts passed a bare city name. A regeneration baked one day's
GeoNames answer into 346 files; a test run compared against another day's. Running
the comparison at four tolerances over one afternoon, on one unchanged tree, gave
2, 6, 63 and 69 failures. No tolerance can be chosen against a target that does not
hold still. The coordinates are frozen in `tests/data/golden_places.py`, and a test
fails if a golden chart reaches for the network.

**Twenty baselines were read by nothing.** The charts that demonstrate the
optional marks — station glyph, out-of-bounds badge, separating aspects, ayanamsa
offset, polar substitution note, relationship score — and three plain natal charts,
generated and committed and compared by no test. They have readers now, and a gate
fails if a stored baseline loses one.

**Two charts wrote the same file.** `save_svg` builds its default name from the
subject's name, so a subject named "John Lennon - Relationship Score" collided with
the explicitly-named file for that same variation and overwrote it. The comparison
test reproduced the loser: a chart with two EMPTY panel rows, compared against a
baseline reading "Relationship Score: 12", passing. A regeneration that overwrites
its own output now stops.

**A regeneration can draw with a different checkout's library.** An editable install
resolves to the path it was installed from, so running a regeneration script from a
git worktree of the same repository uses the ORIGINAL tree's code — including
whatever is uncommitted there — while writing into the worktree. Sixty-four
baselines were produced that way while this release was being written. They were
plausible, idempotent on a second run, and wrong; only a swapped pair of panel rows
gave it away. The scripts check before they write.

**Nine baselines were months stale, and nine were listed as unregenerable.** Four
progression charts, the Ptolemaic BCE pair, the 500 BC progression, and two files
no test read at all — "Ancient Greece 500BC - Synastry Chart" and its transit twin,
exempted from the freshness gate as "second subject not recorded". Their panels
record both subjects: 15 June 500 BC at noon in Athens, and a "Transit Partner"
cast on 1 January 1970. They have readers now, the BCE readers that compared a
±5% line count go through the single comparator, and the exemption table is
empty: every entry was a chart some test casts. The refreshed files carry what the
drawer has changed since they were written — accessibility attributes, palette,
the modern glyph scale, the house-sector arcs. For the other 337 files, the move
from GeoNames to frozen coordinates changed no geometry at all.

### What the review found

A second pass over the branch, before merge, with the tests it added turned on
themselves. The hermetic guard drove one module and skipped every parametrized
test — where twenty-nine of the network calls still were — and ended as skipped on
swisseph because it drove the tests through the real comparison; it drives all
four golden modules now, every case, through a recorder. The reader gate counted
a *mention* as a reader, so deleting the progression tests left it green; it
counts only lines that compare or open the file, and deleting those tests fails
it. Three optional-mark tests asserted a word the chart carried anyway; each now
asserts what only the mark draws. A midpoint composite on a crowded ring declared
no coincident cusps; it does. A hex colour could read as a number; it cannot. A
regeneration on the wrong backend would have been accepted; it is refused. The
sweep over 23 systems cost fifteen seconds; it casts only the angles it reads and
costs two.

A third pass, on the second. The driver behind both gates still passed over five
baseline readers that took class-scoped fixtures, without a word; it reports every
test it cannot call now, the guard holds that list equal to a named allowlist of
tests that compare no baseline, and a class whose setup fails on this kernel is
still checked for what the driver could call. The reader gate's source scan reads
tokens, so a commented-out call cannot vote. The comparator's regeneration path
refuses another checkout's library as the scripts do. A copy of the houses call
kept in the factory "for the Arabic Parts" was unreachable and is gone. And the
counts: twenty unread baselines, not eighteen; equal houses do claim their
Ascendant, and meridian houses their Midheaven.

A fourth. `poe regenerate:svg` refreshed 335 files and left eleven stale — the
progressions, the minified pair, the BCE pairs, which only their tests cast — while
the comparator's failure message recommended exactly that command; it ends by
running those tests with the regeneration variable set. And the field accepted
`[[0, 13]]` while an old payload of a crowded chart declared `[]`: it validates what
it promises, and reads its groups off the cusps when a payload predates it. Every
test the release added was then run against a mutation aimed at it: seventy-odd
runs, two survivors. The hermetic guard passed when no golden chart could be cast
at all, since nothing then reached the network; it now demands that every baseline
within this kernel's and backend's reach was handed to the comparison. And a
reader whose body was emptied still vouched for its file through the source scan;
inside the driven modules the driver is now the only witness.

A fifth, and last. The invariant held against 424,764 charts on each backend and
183,120 more at the minute; what it found was two consumers that refiled an angle
with the reader instead of reading the house the model carries — the house
comparison tables, and the solar-arc direction at an arc of zero — and both read
the model now. `poe check` had never reached its test steps, stopped by a pyright
error in the report generator that predates the branch; it reaches them, and the
extended run of the gates refuses a kernel narrower than the one it asked for.
The eleven test-owned baselines are the only ones that step rewrites. The names
were read once more for what they say.

## The moon that was not beside its name

The bottom-left panel of a chart carries six lines of text and a small disc
drawing the lunar phase. On a natal chart the disc rides above the block,
against the row that names it. On every other chart type it sat ten pixels
under the block's *last* line — while the row naming it was third or fourth
from the end. A transit put the picture 24 pixels and two rows from its own
caption, a return 38 and three. Of the 736 discs drawn across the baselines
and the validation gallery, 113 stood apart from their caption: 95 orphaned
under somebody else's line, and 18 on midpoint composites, whose panel has
never written a phase row at all — a picture that did not say what it depicts.

The phase row now closes the block on every panel that draws a disc, so the
disc beneath it has nothing else it could be captioning. The row moved rather
than the disc because the wheel's chord narrows going up — the top row clears
134px, the last 229 — and a dual panel's phase line carries the wheel's name
too. Measured over 400 combinations (ten languages, five wheel contexts, eight
phases, in the reference fonts' advances): 304 would overrun at the top, 98
overran in the rows the line used to occupy, 21 at the bottom — and those 21
are trimmed now, the way the house and score rows beside them always were,
with the wheel's name paying first so the phase keeps its own words.

Three neighbours were wrong in the same way and are fixed alongside:

- **A dual return read the nativity's moon.** The phase came from the natal
  subject under a bare "Lunar phase", while the row beside it named the two
  wheels apart — the birth's moon read as the return's. It reads the return
  moment now and says so ("Solar Return Lunar phase"); a lunar return drops
  the word the label already carries rather than stuttering.
- **A midpoint composite drew the disc with no words for it.** It no longer
  does — a midpoint composite is not a moment and states no phase — matching
  what the synastry panel, equally short of room, has always done. The phase
  stays on the model for any caller who wants it.
- **A panel with no disc stopped 30px short of the page.** The block now takes
  the room the absent disc would have held, so every panel shares one bottom
  edge.

### The estimator that had never measured Devanagari

The row trims allocate with a per-character width table, measured from three
reference fonts — for Latin, Greek and Cyrillic. Hindi fell to a block ceiling
that charged every code point 1.04 em, matras and viramas included, glyphs
that shaping renders at zero advance: a 159px Hindi return line was read as
342 and cut mid-word in a slot it fit with 70px to spare, the moment the phase
row's trim began to exist. Devanagari is in the measured table now, like
Cyrillic before it — 104 characters at the widest advance the fonts declare,
combining marks at their true zero — and the estimator reads those rows within
2% of the reference advances, still never under. The natal panel's Hindi
lines and the point grid's Hindi names, clipped and abbreviated by the same
over-charge, come back whole with it.

The perspective row turned out to be the one line with no width fitting at
all: moved up a slot by this reshuffle, the Russian apparent-geocentric string
— 198px — ran 19px under the wheel on every Russian dual return, and always
had on transits. It is fitted now like every other row, and to the slot it
*lands* on at the height the block actually sits: blank rows migrate to the
top, disc-less panels drop 30px onto a wider chord, and every solar arc blanks
its diurnality line — three ways a row is drawn somewhere other than where it
was written, each of which had been cutting or freeing width the fit could not
see.

### What the review found here

Four passes: an adversarial panel over the branch, one over each round's own
diff, and a requested CodeRabbit review. The first confirmed the Devanagari
amputation and the unfitted perspective row. The second caught the fix itself
trimming from the template height on the panels that drop — the very
amputation it was written to remove. The third found the shipped behaviour
clean and one latent test weakness. CodeRabbit added the solar-arc case: the
perspective fitted to row 3 by the transit setup lands on row 4 there, still
cut for the narrower chord — the refit now follows the packing. Eight
mutations of the fits and their drops each fail a test; a windowed value built
to overflow every wrong budget and fit only the right one replaced a pin that
rested on 0.15px. The closing measurements, on real type metrics rather than
the estimator that sized the rows: 718 of 718 drawn discs sit against the row
that names them, and across 933 SVGs — baselines, gallery, docs — none of
4,131 panel rows crosses the wheel's chord at its block's true height.

## Upgrading from a87

The compile-time surface only grows. `coincident_house_cusps` is a new optional
field with a default, `houses_ring_with_polar_fallback` and `HouseRing` are new
names beside the two tuple-returning functions whose signatures are unchanged, and
seven helpers move from private to public in `kerykeion.utilities` — four of them
were private copies inside the composite factory, which now uses the shared ones.

Two things a consumer will notice. **The bottom-left panel redraws on every
non-natal chart that names a phase** — the phase row closes the block, the disc
sits against it, a midpoint composite carries no unlabelled disc, and panels
share one bottom edge whether or not a moon is drawn: 131 charts in this
repository's own baselines, positions and wheels untouched. And **an angle
standing on a crowd of identical cusps
now reads as the house it opens.** It fires only where the chart could not have been
right — some 68 charts in 8,280 by a87's own measurement, all under one house system
at extreme latitudes. Every ordinary chart is unchanged, positions and cusps
included.
